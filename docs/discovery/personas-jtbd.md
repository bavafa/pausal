# Personas and jobs to be done

Purpose: describe who Pausal is for and what each person is trying to get done.
Audience: the author.
Status: draft. Last updated: 2026-11-22.

All content here is a hypothesis. No interviews have been run. Each persona becomes "validated" only after the interview plan in `interview-guide.md` is completed.

## 1. Streaming platform ad product owner
- Job: "When I own the pause ad format, I want ads that fit the scene, so that viewers accept the format and advertisers see value."
- Pain (hypothesis): matching ads to a scene at the pause moment must be fast, safe and explainable.
- Success (hypothesis): a high share of pauses with a relevant ad, zero unsafe ads.

## 2. Brand advertiser or agency planner
- Job: "When I buy a pause placement, I want to know the scene is suitable and relevant, so that my brand is not shown in the wrong moment."
- Pain (hypothesis): little transparency on why an ad appeared.
- Success (hypothesis): clear suppression rules and a reason for each shown or withheld ad.

## 3. Viewer
- Job: "When I pause to take a break, I want nothing that feels like an interruption, and sometimes a product I actually care about."
- Pain (hypothesis): irrelevant or badly timed ads in the middle of a sad or tense scene.
- Success (hypothesis): quiet by default, relevant when shown, easy to dismiss.

## 4. Streaming platform (the business)
- Job: "When a viewer pauses, I want to turn that idle moment into revenue without hurting the viewing experience, so that ad income grows and subscribers stay."
- Revenue job (hypothesis): sell pause inventory at a price that reflects how well the ad fits the scene. Better fit should support a higher price and a higher chance of action.
- Cost and risk (hypothesis): an unsafe or irrelevant ad can cost advertiser trust and viewer goodwill, so withholding an ad is sometimes the better business decision.

## 5. Publisher or content owner (the studio or channel whose content is paused)
- Job: "When ads run on my content, I want a share of the revenue and control over what appears next to it, so that I earn from the audience without damaging the content."
- Revenue job (hypothesis): earn a revenue share per pause ad shown. A no-ad outcome earns nothing, so the cost of caution must be visible.
- Pain (hypothesis): scenes that matter to the story (grief, tension) are the ones where an ad does the most harm.

## 6. Advertiser (the seller of the product)
- Job: "When I pay for a pause placement, I want my product shown in a moment where it fits and the scene is suitable, so that my money produces attention and sales."
- Revenue job (hypothesis): maximize return on ad spend. Pay more for a better-fitting moment, pay nothing for a wasted or unsafe one.

## Revenue rules to test (all simulated, all hypotheses)
1. Price per pause ad rises with the bid and with the relevance score.
2. No ad is shown, and no revenue is earned, when relevance is below the threshold or the scene is not brand safe.
3. The slider moves the ranking between pure relevance (viewer and advertiser fit) and pure revenue (platform and publisher income). The demo shows what each setting costs in relevance.
4. Revenue split between platform and publisher is an open question (see the brief).
